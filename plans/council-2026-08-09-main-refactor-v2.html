LLM Council · Verdict

Should Nova Play commit to main-refactor-v2.md?

Three-app codebase — webOS TV, Android TV, Android phone — on a shared @nova/engine, across nine gated phases. Judged against constraints the plan did not know: two people, 40 hrs/week total, one Android TV box, one test phone, real users on the shipping webOS app, phone required.

Verdict: kill v2 as written. The plan is rigorous but rigorous about the wrong thing — it makes architecture the critical path to a required product, and puts the phone at Phase 7 behind a Phase 6 gate that is explicitly allowed to say “Defer/stop phone.” Build the phone first, prove Android playback in week one, and let the shared engine be earned by duplication rather than assumed up front.

Where the council agrees

  • Do not commit to v2 as written. The phone does not begin until Phase 7 — after guard infrastructure, requirements validation, the playback spike, engine extraction, an Android TV product, the Phase 5 apps/webos move, and Phase 6’s experiment.
  • Phone before Android TV. Phase 4’s Android TV slice is an unjustified detour ahead of the one product that is actually required. Reconsider Android TV only once the phone ships and its support burden is known.
  • Run the Android playback canary before Phase 0. spikes/android-playback/ moves no shipping source and its own rollback section says it cannot damage webOS. Phase 0’s target-registry.mjs, workspace-aware cycle resolution, CSS scanner parameterization and six guard fixtures all protect source movement the canary does not perform.
  • A D-pad TV interface is not an honest phone product. Touch navigation, keyboard search, system Back, orientation, accessibility and phone-owned player controls are the real work.
  • Staying webOS-only is rejected. Android is a stated business requirement.

Where the council clashes

The thin WebView wrapper. The Contrarian and the Executor called it a disposable diagnostic only. First Principles, the Expansionist and the Outsider treated it as the seed of the shipping product — First Principles went furthest: “phone first, and it is nearly free.” Four of five peer reviewers picked that line out as the single biggest blind spot in the round.

Chairman’s resolution: the wrapper is a mandatory diagnostic and a keepable shell, but never the product. Package today’s web output unmodified, prove playback and lifecycle, retain the Capacitor shell if WebView wins — then replace the phone-friction surfaces with touch-first presentation. Passing the media corpus proves a backend, not readiness: the Phase 2 spike by design has no catalog and no provider implementation, so it says nothing about login, credentials, persistence, accessibility or distribution.

Second clash: a permanently separate Android codebase. Four advisors endorsed it without pricing the maintenance cost. For two people, two divergent implementations of provider, playback, catalog and policy logic is exactly the cost @nova/engine and verify:single-engine existed to prevent. The resolution is a distinct phone app and presentation, not a permanent logic fork — share pure modules incrementally, when both products consume them, and never as a precondition to shipping.

Blind spots the peer review caught

The device gate cannot be satisfied honestly. Four of five reviewers raised this independently — the strongest signal in the round, and no advisor had mentioned it. Phase 1 §4 demands approved minimum and current device classes; Phases 2, 4 and 7 require every physical-device row to pass. One phone and one TV box establishes feasibility on those two devices, not Android compatibility. The choice must be named on Monday, not deferred: a controlled beta (est. 8–12 testers across ≥3 OEM/API/WebView classes, with sanitized crash/lifecycle/backend telemetry and rollback), or support explicitly limited to the named tested phone/API/WebView combination. A one-row “supported matrix” is fiction.

Capacity was never converted into a budget. Reserve 8–12 hrs/week for webOS support and release; that leaves 28–32 hrs/week for Android. A real phone MVP is estimated at 240–320 engineering hours excluding device certification — roughly 9–13 calendar weeks. Not “nearly free,” not “install now.”

The calendar is the real killer. Section 7 refuses to estimate effort or dates. Green gates can consume months while delivering nothing installable.

Orchestrator check — one review claim was wrong, and it makes the plan cheaper

Reviewer 2 argued that nobody had verified the existing build even runs outside webOS, and that platform coupling — not codecs — might be the real risk. That claim would change the Monday action, so it was checked against the repository instead of passed through.

It is largely false. isWebOsRuntime() (src/main.ts:350) is a pure feature detector for window.webOSSystem || window.PalmSystem. All 34 webOS references in main.ts are either ternary branches on that boolean (:378, :382, :386, :4705, :4711, :4945) or optional-chained calls that no-op when the bridge is absent (system?.keepAlive?.(true) at :8599 and :8617). Launch params early-return when absent (:403–407). The app is developed daily in a desktop browser via npm run dev — the same environment as a bare Capacitor WebView.

Corollary: the Chromium 79 / ES2015 target is a floor, not a ceiling. Any modern Android System WebView runs that output. The canary needs no build changes at all — it can package the existing webos-app/ output unmodified. Reviewer 2’s second point does stand, and fed the chairman: the separate-app path commits two people to divergent codebases.

The recommendation

Replace v2’s nine phases with three delivery checkpoints:

  1. Playback decision. Answer only the subset of docs/android/requirements.md needed for mandatory phone protocols, cleartext policy, lifecycle, tracks, cancellation and the legal corpus. Run WebView first. Record sanitized results in docs/android/playback-spike-results.json; commit docs/adr/android-playback-backend.md. If WebView fails, a Media3 feasibility spike is est. 24–32 hours (full plugin integration remains unestimated). If neither backend passes the mandatory rows, stop at that ADR commit and change requirements rather than touching webOS.
  2. Phone vertical slice. Credentials and provider connectivity, browse/search, playback, real track switching, A-to-B cancellation, resume, storage, background/foreground recovery, system Back, touch controls. Do not create modules/engine or apps/android-tv, and do not run verify:boundaries or verify:single-engine first.
  3. Controlled release. Signing, distribution, redacted telemetry, accessibility checks, and the controlled device beta. Extract a shared module only when you are otherwise fixing the same logic twice — and at that first extraction add the smallest necessary workspace guard, not all of Phase 0 preemptively.

Delete Phase 5 (moving webOS into apps/webos delivers no capability and destabilizes the only shipping app). Delete Phase 6 — the phone is not optional, so a gate permitting “Defer/stop phone” is incoherent with the requirement.

Kill criteria, set now: two consecutive weeks with no APK completing login → browse → playback; more than 25% of capacity spent on verification machinery; or no end-to-end phone slice after 160 engineering hours. The abandonment checkpoint is the playback ADR commit, before any Phase 3 workspace commit — at that boundary the untouched webOS release is still intact and scope can reduce to the phone alone.

The one thing to do first

On Monday: tag the current approved webOS commit, then create one isolated commit under spikes/android-phone-playback/.

Spend ~4 hours fixing the mandatory phone playback/lifecycle requirements and the legal corpus. Then ~20–28 hours packaging today’s unmodified webos-app/ output in the smallest Capacitor shell and installing it on the owned phone. Test HLS, DASH, MPEG-TS, real audio and subtitle switching, A-cancel-then-B, background/foreground, Activity recreation, process death, and cleartext/CORS behaviour.

Commit the sanitized report and the backend ADR. Do not start scripts/target-registry.mjs, test:guards, apps/webos, modules/engine or Android TV before that evidence exists.

Peer review round

Five anonymized reviews — what the convergence was hidingsol·xhigh, opus-4-8-think·high, terra·high, luna·high, terra·xhigh

Each reviewer saw all five responses as A–E with identities stripped, plus the framing and the v2 plan. Map: Executor = A, Outsider = B, Contrarian = C, Expansionist = D, First Principles = E.

Strongest response

Four of five picked A (the Executor) — the only response converting the shared diagnosis into an executable schedule with hour budgets, falsifiable warning signs, and named things to delete. One picked C (the Contrarian) for the clearest product verdict, one picked D (the Expansionist) for the cleanest diagnostic-versus-product distinction.

Biggest blind spot

Unanimous: E (First Principles), for “phone first, and it is nearly free.” Reviewer 3: “reckless nonsense. A passing media corpus proves playback feasibility, not a shippable phone product… Phase 2 explicitly has no catalog/provider implementation.” Reviewer 5 added that “WebView fails, therefore Media3” does not follow either — Media3 still requires a native plugin, lifecycle integration and track/control parity.

What all five advisors missed

Device coverage (4 of 5 reviewers, independently). Every advisor silently accepted one-phone testing as sufficient Android evidence. Reviewer 1: “one successful handset cannot justify an ‘Android phone’ release or A’s 8–10-week forecast.” Reviewer 3: “v2’s Phase 1 matrix is presently either dishonest (one row per form factor) or impossible… otherwise ‘Android is a business requirement’ is not an executable commitment, and every promised phone schedule is fiction.”

Capacity as a release constraint (Reviewer 4). Nobody converted 40 total hours/week into an allocation across webOS support, Android development, QA, release and user support — which makes the 8–10 week claims unsupported.

Divergent-codebase cost (Reviewer 2). A separate app commits two people to permanently maintaining divergent codebases — precisely the cost v2’s single engine existed to prevent. Nobody did that arithmetic. This one survived into the chairman’s verdict.

webOS coupling (Reviewer 2). Claimed the existing build might not run outside webOS at all. Checked against the repo and found largely false — see the orchestrator note above. The canary is cheaper than the reviewer feared.
